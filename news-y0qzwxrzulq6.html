Anime, Games & Kawaii — Your Choice of Yokohama, or Shibuya & Harajuku

Choose your area — 3 hours of gachapon, crane games, and Pokémon cards, done not just watched

Which gachapon machine to try, how to actually win at a crane game, what's even on the shelves at a card shop — both Yokohama and Shibuya/Harajuku run deep enough to fill a full day on their own, but for a first-time visitor, that depth is exactly what gets in the way. Pick whichever area suits you, and three hours with an English-speaking guide clears that barrier, turning it into time spent actually playing.

01




01｜Yokohama, or Shibuya & Harajuku — Which to Choose
Rows of gachapon machines inside a Yokohama arcade
Yokohama suits those who'd rather explore arcades and card shops at a calmer pace. Shibuya and Harajuku, on the other hand, suit those who want the energy and crowds of Japan's pop culture epicenter itself. Neither is the "right" choice — the area is decided together with your guide beforehand, based on your mood and the rest of your itinerary.





02｜Learning How Gachapon and Crane Games Actually Work
Rows of gachapon machines inside a Yokohama arcade
Gachapon machines that look identical at a glance often differ in odds and series lineup, and crane games vary just as much depending on the shop and the claw itself. The guide explains the mechanics on the spot, then gives you a real shot at winning. Even a miss is part of the experience — arcade culture included.

03｜Trading Cards and Purikura
Inside a card shop, packs lined up on display
Shops selling Pokémon cards and other trading cards can be hard to navigate without reading Japanese. The guide walks you through how the shelves are organized and how to open a pack, and one Pokémon Card booster pack per guest is included. The day closes with purikura, the guide joining you for both the shoot and the editing — a keepsake unlike an ordinary photo.

What the tour handles for you

Choose Yokohama, or Shibuya and Harajuku, as your setting. An English-speaking guide throughout, one gachapon play, one crane game play, one Japanese sweet, and one Pokémon Card booster pack per guest are all included. Shopping purchases are not included. The tour moves entirely on foot, so no transport costs apply. It runs for a minimum of 2 and maximum of 10 guests, with bookings required at least 30 days in advance. Transfers from a hotel or other location are available on request.
