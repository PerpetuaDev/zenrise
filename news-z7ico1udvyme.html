A Day of Living, Not Just Staying, in Kamakura

Stay in a newly built house. 5 minutes to the sea, 10 minutes to the station, free parking

Kamakura has two faces — the one built around temple visits and sightseeing, and the quieter one lived day to day, shaped by the sea breeze. This newly built house leans toward the latter. A fully private guest space is set aside, independent from the host's own living area apart from the entrance. It sits just 5 minutes from the sea and 10 minutes from Kamakura Station, tucked into a quiet residential street.

01





01｜A Stay Built Around Living, Not Just Visiting
The private guest living space
The host lives on the second floor of the same building, but every space beyond the entrance — the bathroom, toilet, kitchen, and washer-dryer — belongs entirely to the guest. By design, it's possible to go through the whole stay barely crossing paths with the host. It feels less like a hotel room and more like renting a house in Kamakura for a few days of your own.




02｜A New House, Close to Both Sea and Station
The interior, the wood grain of Sumitomo Forestry construction still fresh
Built by Sumitomo Forestry, the house still carries the scent of new wood. Yuigahama Beach is a 5-minute walk away, Kamakura Station 10 minutes, with Wadazuka Station also nearby. A convenience store, supermarket, kaiten-zushi restaurant, café, shabu-shabu spot, and Kua'aina are all within a 3-minute walk. Free parking is included, making it just as workable for guests arriving by car.

03｜A Koto Performance, If You'd Like One
The host's hands at the koto
On request, guests can enjoy a roughly 30-minute koto performance by the host — a quiet piece of Japanese music woven into the stay itself, the kind of cultural moment that's hard to reach through sightseeing alone. Guests hoping to hear it are best off mentioning it by message ahead of time.

What the stay offers you

A private bathroom, kitchen, and washer-dryer, free parking, and — on request — a roughly 30-minute koto performance are all part of the stay. Check-in generally runs from 3:00 PM to 7:00 PM, with some variation by day of the week. There's a shower only, no bathtub, and the property is entirely smoke-free with no pets allowed. As it sits in a quiet residential neighborhood, guests are asked to be mindful of the surroundings.

Our own tours

We are a local travel specialist focused on Kamakura, Shonan, and Yokohama. Alongside stays like this one — built around living in Kamakura rather than simply visiting it — we help shape days around the Zen Journey, our guide service, and the rest of what the area has to offer.
